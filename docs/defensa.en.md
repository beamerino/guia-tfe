# The defence

Your defence isn't an exam on everything you know. It's your chance to **explain in a few minutes what problem you tackled, what you did and what you achieved**. If your thesis follows the [universal structure](estructura.md), your presentation already has its script.

!!! note "Length and format"
    Presentation time, question time and whether the defence is in person or online depend on your university. It's usually 10–20 minutes of presentation. **Check your regulations** and adjust everything below.

## Presentation structure

Follow the same order as the thesis, with more weight on what you did yourself:

| Part | Indicative slides (for about 15 min) |
|---|---|
| Title | 1 |
| Problem and motivation | 1–2 |
| State of the art and the gap you fill | 1–2 |
| Main and secondary objectives | 1 |
| Materials and methods | 2–3 |
| Results (one per method step) | 3–4 |
| Discussion and limitations | 1–2 |
| Conclusions and future work | 1 |
| Thank you / questions | 1 |

Rule of thumb: **about one slide per minute**.

## Slides that help

- **One idea per slide**, with a title that states it: "Model B beats model A on sensitivity" rather than "Results 2".
- **Little text.** If you're going to read it, don't put it on the slide. Use figures, diagrams and summary tables.
- **Large text** (at least 18–20 pt) and figures readable from the back of the room.
- **Number** your slides: the committee will use the numbers to ask questions ("on slide 9…").
- Show your **objective** again when you reach the conclusions, so it's clear you met it.
- Prepare **backup slides** at the end (after "Thank you") with details you might be asked about.

## Rehearse

- **Rehearse aloud with a timer**, at least three full times. Running over time is one of the things that costs most.
- **Rehearse in front of someone**, ideally from outside your field.
- **Record yourself** once: you'll notice filler words, pace and where you lose the thread.
- If you do a **demo**, also bring a **recorded video** in case something fails live.
- Bring the presentation **as a PDF and in its original format**, on your laptop and on a USB stick or in the cloud.

## Committee questions

They're predictable. Prepare a short answer for each of these:

- Why did you choose this topic and this approach?
- Why this methodology and not another?
- What's the main limitation of your work?
- How do your results compare with the state of the art?
- What would you do differently if you started again?
- Is it applicable in practice? What's missing for it to be?
- Specific questions about a figure, table or technical decision.

How to answer:

- **Listen to the whole question**, thank them if you like, and think for a second before answering.
- **Answer what you're asked**, briefly. If you don't understand the question, ask them to repeat it.
- **If you don't know, say so**: "I didn't analyse that, but I would approach it like this…" is worth far more than making something up.
- **Criticism isn't an attack.** Acknowledge limitations calmly: they're already in your discussion.
