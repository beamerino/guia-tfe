# Choosing and narrowing your topic

A good final project is not the one that promises the most. It is the one that **delivers what it promises**. Almost every problem I see halfway through the year comes from a topic that was not narrowed down at the start.

!!! error-tipico "The most common mistake"
    Setting **many, very ambitious objectives** instead of focusing on one specific objective and asking yourself: *can I really achieve this?*

    A project with five half-finished objectives is worth less than one with a single objective done well.

## From topic to objective

A **topic** is an area ("AI in cardiology"). An **objective** is something you will do and that can be checked when you finish. To get from one to the other:

1. **Start from a problem, not a tool.** "I want to use deep learning" is not a problem. "ECGs in primary care are reviewed late" is.
2. **Look at what has already been done.** A first quick search (see [Literature review](estado-del-arte.md)) will tell you whether the problem is already solved, where the gap is and what is realistic.
3. **Write a single main objective** with a verb whose outcome can be checked: *design, develop, evaluate, compare, validate, analyse*. Avoid vague verbs such as *improve, study, explore* or *investigate*.
4. **Break the main objective into 2–4 secondary objectives.** Each one will become a step in your method and will have its own result. See [Thesis structure](estructura.md).
5. **Run it through the feasibility test** below.

## Before and after

| Too ambitious | Focused and feasible |
|---|---|
| Develop an AI system that improves the diagnosis of cardiovascular disease in primary care. | Evaluate the performance of two machine learning models for detecting atrial fibrillation in a public ECG dataset. |
| Create a mental health app for university students. | Design a prototype mood-tracking app and evaluate its usability with a group of 10–15 users. |
| Study the use of AI in hospitals. | Carry out a scoping review of generative AI applications in clinical documentation published between 2020 and 2025. |
| Optimise a company's logistics with AI. | Compare three demand forecasting algorithms using two years of a company's historical sales data. |

Notice what the focused versions have in common: **a verb you can check, a defined scope** (which data, which population, which period) and **something that can be measured**.

## Feasibility test

Before you settle on your objective, answer honestly:

- **Time:** does it fit into the hours you have? One ECTS credit is 25–30 hours of work. A 12-ECTS bachelor's project is about 300–360 hours, including writing.
- **Data:** do you already have the data, or access to it? If you depend on a permission, an ethics committee or a company, will it arrive in time?
- **Tools:** do you know how to use them, or how long will it take you to learn?
- **Evaluation:** how will you know at the end whether you have succeeded? With which metric, test or criterion?
- **Plan B:** if the main part fails (the data doesn't arrive, the model doesn't converge), which part of the work still stands on its own?

If any answer is "I don't know", **narrow it further**. It is always easier to extend a small project than to rescue a huge one.

!!! clave "Rule of thumb"
    If you can't state your main objective in **a single sentence** and say **how you will check** that you've achieved it, it isn't focused enough yet.

## What to bring to our first meeting

- One sentence describing the **problem** you want to address.
- A draft **main objective** and 2–4 secondary ones.
- **3–5 relevant references** you have found.
- What **data and tools** you have or need.
