# Writing and style

You don't need to write beautifully. You need to write **clearly, precisely and consistently**. A committee that understands what you did on first reading will value your work far more.

## Basic principles

- **One idea per paragraph.** The first sentence says what the paragraph is about; the rest develops it.
- **Short sentences.** If a sentence runs over three lines, it's probably two.
- **Precision over decoration.** "Performance improved" → "sensitivity rose from 78% to 86%".
- **No unsupported opinions.** Avoid "obviously", "it is well known", "clearly" or "revolutionary". If it's true, give the data or the citation.
- **Consistent voice.** Choose impersonal ("was designed") or first-person plural ("we designed") and keep it throughout. Check whether your university or supervisor prefers one.
- **Tenses.** What you did (methods and results) goes in the **past**. What is generally true (what the literature says, what a figure shows) goes in the **present**.

## Terms and acronyms

- **Define each acronym the first time** it appears: "electrocardiogram (ECG)". Then use only the acronym.
- If you use many, include a **list of acronyms** at the start.
- **One concept, one term.** If you call it "model", don't later call it "system", "algorithm" and "tool" as if they were the same.

## Figures and tables

- **All numbered, with a title or caption** that explains what they show without needing the text.
- **All referred to in the text** before they appear: "As Figure 3 shows…".
- **Table captions go above**; **figure captions go below**.
- **Readable**: labelled axes with units, large enough text, no blurry screenshots.
- If a figure or table isn't yours, **cite the source** in the caption ("Adapted from [12]").
- **Don't repeat** every number from a table in the text: highlight what matters.

## Numbers and units

- Use the decimal separator of your language consistently (a point in English: 0.85; a comma in Spanish: 0,85).
- Leave a space between number and unit: 10 ms, 3.2 GB.
- Don't give more decimals than make sense: 0.8534 → 0.85.

## Common mistakes

!!! error-tipico "What I correct most often"
    - One-sentence paragraphs, or paragraphs a page long.
    - Citations at the end of a paragraph, with no way to tell which sentence they support.
    - Mixing results and interpretation in the results chapter.
    - Figures that are never mentioned.
    - Undefined acronyms.
    - Copying and pasting from papers and changing four words. That isn't paraphrasing, and plagiarism checkers catch it.
    - Not proofreading: typos, cut-off sentences, broken cross-references ("see Figure ??").

## How to revise your own text

1. **Let the text rest** for at least a day before revising.
2. **Read it aloud** or with a screen reader: mistakes jump out.
3. **Revise in layers**: first structure (is everything in the right chapter?), then paragraphs, and finally typos.
4. **Use the spell checker** in Word or Overleaf and, if you like, style tools. If you use [generative AI](ia.md) to revise, use it to point out problems, not to rewrite your work.
5. Ask someone who **doesn't know your topic** to read the abstract and introduction. If they don't understand them, they need clarifying.
