# Planning

Time runs out sooner than you think. Almost nobody finishes late for lack of ability. It happens because they leave the writing and the reviews until the end.

## Plan backwards

Start from your university's **submission deadline** and work backwards:

1. **Submission**: final document, signed and in the required format.
2. **Final review by your supervisor**: ask for it at least 2–3 weeks before submission. They need time to read it and you need time to make corrections.
3. **Complete draft**: every chapter written, even if it still needs polishing.
4. **Results closed**: from this point on, no more experiments.
5. **Development and experiments**: the longest block.
6. **Literature review and methodology defined**.
7. **Objective approved**: your starting point.

!!! clave "Keep 20% in reserve"
    Something will go wrong: the data arrives late, the code breaks, you fall ill. If your plan has no slack, any setback forces you to postpone.

## An indicative timeline

For a project of about 4–5 months (adjust to your case):

| Phase | Indicative weeks |
|---|---|
| Topic and focused objective | 1–2 |
| Literature search and screening | 3–5 |
| Writing the related work | 6–7 |
| Materials and methods (in parallel) | 6–7 |
| Development and experiments | 8–13 |
| Results and discussion | 14–15 |
| Full draft review | 16–17 |
| Corrections and submission | 18 |
| Preparing the defence | 19–20 |

How much work it is in hours: each ECTS credit equals 25–30 hours. Some examples:

| Credits | Approximate hours |
|---|---|
| 6 ECTS | 150–180 h |
| 12 ECTS | 300–360 h |
| 15 ECTS | 375–450 h |
| 30 ECTS | 750–900 h |

Check the exact credits for your project in your study plan and divide the hours by the weeks you have. That tells you how many hours a week you really need.

## Write from the start

- **The thesis isn't written at the end.** Write each chapter as you finish that phase: related work after the literature review, methods while you apply them.
- **Keep a work log**: what you did, what you decided and why, what failed. It will save you hours when writing the methods and give you answers for the defence.
- **Keep versions and backups.** Use version control (Git) for code and a synced folder for the document. "I lost the file" is not an excuse that works.

## Send drafts in parts

Sending the whole document in the week of submission leaves no time to fix anything. Send it by chapter or block, in this order:

1. Objectives and structure (annotated table of contents).
2. Related work.
3. Materials and methods.
4. Results and discussion.
5. Introduction, conclusions and abstract (written last).

## When you get stuck

- **Technical block** (something doesn't work): put a time limit on it. If you've spent more than 2–3 days on the same thing, ask for help or find another route.
- **Writer's block**: don't start with the introduction. Start with the methods, which is what you know best.
- **Scope block** (you won't make it): go back to the [feasibility test](tema.md#feasibility-test) and cut. Cutting in time is good project management, not failure.
